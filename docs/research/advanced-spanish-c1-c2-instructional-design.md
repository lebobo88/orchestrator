# Advanced Spanish (C1-C2) Instructional Design: External Evidence for the Lizbeth Spanish Advanced Band

## Purpose and how this briefing was commissioned

This briefing was commissioned to pressure-test the first-pass Advanced-band syllabus draft
(`lizbeth_spanish/curriculum/advanced-band-syllabus.md`, commit ba2cbd9) against fresh external
evidence before that draft was revised into its current, superseding version. It joins two existing
project research documents as the project's research corpus for this course: `docs/research/Global
Spanish–English Language and Translation Training Ecosystems: Curriculum Architecture, Dialect
Matrices, and Market Strategy.md` (the anchor research) and `docs/research/inclusive-spanish-usage.md`.
This briefing does not replace either; it extends both with C1/C2-specific instructional-design
evidence neither anchor document addresses in comparable depth.

Audience: the instructor (course owner, for approval-stage confidence) and future Phase 6 content
authors of the Advanced band (for citation and grounding when they write each week's actual lesson
content).

**Authority boundary.** This briefing is advisory research evidence. It does not authorize any change
to project scope, does not approve any engineering work, and does not itself decide open instructor
questions; it supplies evidence that the revised syllabus (`advanced-band-syllabus.md`) and the
plan-of-record (`docs/plans/lizbeth-spanish-advanced-band.md`) draw on, cite, and act within their own,
separately approved scope. Where this briefing's findings bear on an open instructor decision, that
decision is named and left open in the citing documents, not resolved here.

## Decision context

The Advanced-band syllabus draft named five categories of instructional-design uncertainty that this
briefing was asked to investigate:

1. Official CEFR guidance and third-party contact-hour benchmarks for C1/C2, to sanity-check the
   draft's 13/13-week C1/C2 pacing default and this course's own contact-hour scale.
2. Multi-week unit-continuity mechanics: whether any external framework specifies how a "content-based
   and project-driven" multi-week unit should actually work inside a fixed weekly lesson template, the
   single largest open item the draft itself flagged.
3. Competitor and comparable-model context: how other Spanish-language platforms and institutional
   frameworks handle C1/C2 content, for instructor context on this project's own ambition level.
4. Diachronic/historical-register sourceability: whether the draft's old/modern (Golden Age
   literature, archaic legal register) mechanism has genuine external grounding, and whether the
   specific readability tool it names (LECOLE) actually covers archaic text.
5. Core-band interpreting/translation depth: whether the draft's C1/C2 interpreting content
   (structured consecutive/simultaneous interpreting, advanced conference interpreting) is
   appropriately scoped for a general course, or borrows depth from professional-certification
   sources without saying so.

Each sub-decision below follows the What/So-what/Now-what structure: what the evidence shows, what it
means for the Advanced-band syllabus, and what should be validated or decided next.

## Sub-decision A: CEFR official guidance and contact-hour benchmarks

**What.** The CEFR Global Scale (Table 1, CEFR 3.3) states the C1 and C2 descriptors the anchor
research already paraphrases correctly [S1, high confidence]. The CEFR Companion Volume (2018/2020)
added an expanded mediation framework of 24 illustrative mediation scales, with descriptors enriched
specifically at the C levels [S2, moderate confidence — the Companion Volume's primary PDF returned an
HTTP 403 on fetch attempt; this claim rests on secondary academic summaries, not a verbatim primary
reading]. The CEFR's "translating a written text" mediation scale is explicitly stated as not intended
to relate to professional translators or their training [S3, moderate confidence]. No official Council
of Europe per-level guided-hour figures exist. Widely cited estimates (roughly 700-800 cumulative hours
through C1; 1000-1200 cumulative hours through C2; 150-400-plus hours for a single upper-level jump)
are Cambridge/EF-lineage exam-board estimates, not Council of Europe-official figures, and they
consistently show each successive CEFR level requiring more hours than the one before it [S4, moderate
confidence].

**So what.** The syllabus draft's confirmed band-to-CEFR mapping (Advanced = C1 then C2) is unaffected;
that mapping is instructor-confirmed independently of hour estimates. The unconfirmed 13/13-week
internal pacing split gains a mild directional caveat: since C2 tends to require more hours than C1
generically, an even split is not strongly supported by outside benchmarks, though it is not
contradicted either — this is a weak signal, not a reason to change the default. Separately, and more
consequentially: this course's own contact-hour scale (approximately 26 one-hour lessons per CEFR
level) is roughly two orders of magnitude below the generic per-level guided-hour benchmarks cited
above. This gap was not previously flagged anywhere in the project's curriculum documentation. The
expanded Companion Volume mediation framework gives more precise grounding for what the five-pillar
translation/mediation column can concretely mean at C1/C2 than the anchor research's own generic
mention supplies. The "translating a written text" scale's explicit non-professional-translator framing
independently supports treating light translation/mediation as appropriate general C1/C2 content,
distinct from professional-certification depth.

**Now what.** The revised syllabus keeps the 13/13 split as an unconfirmed working default but records
the directional caveat and recommends instructor confirmation before Phase 6 authoring locks the C2
weeks. It also adds a new, previously absent flagged item: an explicit "curated/accelerated
instructor-led program, not a claim of matching generic guided-hour benchmarks" positioning statement,
which the instructor should confirm as the intended framing. The Companion Volume's C-level mediation
scales are recommended as the more precise reference point for Phase 6 authors writing the
translation/mediation column, with the stated primary-source access caveat carried forward.

## Sub-decision B: multi-week unit-continuity mechanics (the biggest open item)

**What.** Four independent frameworks bear on this question. Project-Based Language Learning (PBLL),
per the University of Hawai'i National Foreign Language Resource Center's authoritative definition,
describes a project spanning "a few weeks to a full semester" as the main part of a unit, organized
around a single driving question, converging on a cumulative public product [S5, high confidence]. Its
defining continuity mechanic is a genuinely cumulative, evolving deliverable, not merely a shared theme
or vocabulary set. Task-Based Language Teaching (TBLT) treats multi-week extension as optional
("analogous to project-based learning" when extended); single-session task cycles remain its standard
unit [S6, moderate confidence]. Content and Language Integrated Learning (CLIL) modules range from a
single unit to an entire semester; a documented peer-reviewed pilot used a flexible 6-week module, 2-3
lessons per week over 6-8 weeks [S7, moderate-high confidence] — the closest documented real-world
analog to the draft's 4-6-week arcs. Instituto Cervantes' own flagship AVE Global platform structures
its C1 level as four sub-courses (C1.1-C1.4), each built from roughly three topics of approximately 10
hours each [S8, high confidence] — a real institutional answer using bounded, roughly 10-hour topic
blocks, not single lessons and not one continuous multi-month project.

**So what.** The convergent evidence (CLIL's 6-8-week modules; Cervantes' approximately 10-hour topic
blocks) supports a bounded multi-week thematic block in the same range the draft's four domain arcs
already occupy (4-6 weeks each). This is a defensible reading of "content-based and project-driven
design." Critically, no source reviewed prescribes a template-level continuity mechanic (a shared
running file, a modified per-week structure, or a cumulative tracked artifact) as a requirement for
achieving this kind of bounded thematic block; TBLT, CLIL, and AVE Global all achieve multi-week
coherence through content sequencing and topic grouping, not through a structural template change. True
PBLL fidelity, by contrast, specifically requires a genuinely cumulative evolving deliverable — a
materially stronger and more structurally demanding claim the other three frameworks do not require and
this course's existing template does not currently support without modification.

**Now what.** This is the central resolution the revised syllabus adopts: "content-based/
project-driven" is realized as a content style (one named recurring scenario per arc, narrated within
the existing `practice-activity.md` file and consolidated at each arc's existing `weekly-review-recap.md`),
requiring no new file type and no change to `LessonContentParser`, the PPTX/PDF/TypeScript generators,
the test suite, or the app's `import.meta.glob` week-discovery. The one instructor decision this
briefing surfaces but does not resolve: if the instructor instead wants true PBLL fidelity (a single
cumulative, tracked deliverable across an arc), that is a distinct, build-affecting choice requiring a
scoped engineering change and a new or amended plan, not something either this briefing or the syllabus
revision assumes.

## Sub-decision C: competitor and comparable-model context

**What.** Instituto Cervantes' own Plan Curricular (the C1-C2 volume) strongly confirms a
professional/academic/literary C1-C2 orientation: texts for a specialized-education reader, abstract
topics treated extensively, literary or non-literary [S9, high confidence]. AVE Global, the same
institution's flagship platform, offers no C2 course at all — it stops at C1.4 [S8, high confidence].
SIELE, a major Spanish-proficiency certification instrument, issues no C2 rating, reported as
challenging to test with software in a single adaptive instrument spanning A1-C1 [S10, moderate-high
confidence]. DELE does certify C2, but with a heavier, more integrated-skills format than its C1 exam
[S11, moderate confidence]. Lightly-sourced secondary evidence also shows university advanced-Spanish
programs commonly pairing advanced grammar with literary/cultural analysis and debate/presentation work
[S12, low-moderate confidence], and commercial business-Spanish tracks centering on negotiation
role-play and presentation modules [S13, low confidence], both broadly consistent with, but not adding
new specifics to, this project's existing business framework.

**So what.** The project's C1-C2 professional/academic/literary orientation is well-supported by the
market leader's own curricular framework. At the same time, the observable market pattern among
comparable major platforms (AVE Global, SIELE) is to under-build or omit C2 entirely, likely because
formalizing assessment and content at that level is harder. This project's commitment to a fully
symmetric, fully-authored C2 half (already committed under the governing plan's decision D5) is
therefore more ambitious than the observable market pattern, not merely equivalent to it.

**Now what.** This is useful instructor context, not a reason to reduce scope; the governing plan's
decision D5 already commits to full-scale, up-front authoring of both C1 and C2, and this briefing
finds no evidence that argues against that commitment. It is recorded in the revised syllabus as
informational context.

## Sub-decision D: diachronic/historical-register sourceability

**What.** LECOLE, a peer-reviewed readability-management algorithm published in XLinguae (2024), is
real and correctly cited by the anchor research; it is validated with better than 80% correlation, but
only on contemporary Instituto Cervantes/DELE texts — no source reviewed extends or validates it for
archaic or Golden Age Spanish [S14, high confidence]. GALGO (Golden Age Literature Glossary Online),
published in Digital Humanities Quarterly, confirms real scholarly infrastructure exists for glossing
Golden Age Spanish readability, independent of LECOLE [S15, moderate-high confidence]. Graded-reader
pedagogy at B2+ conventionally favors lightly adapted, shorter literary fiction with glosses over
unabridged canonical text; shorter Cervantes Novelas Ejemplares-style stories are more manageable than
Don Quixote, whose archaic language is documented as difficult even at C1 [S16, moderate confidence].
Archaic legal/notarial Spanish teaching is real and has archival precedent (legal-Spanish courses at
B2-C2; the Library of Congress's "Herencia" historical notarial archive) [S17, moderate confidence], but
these are specialist, not general-course, offerings; no source reviewed shows a direct precedent for
pairing archaic Spanish legal register with a parallel archaic English-side legal register inside one
general (non-specialist) advanced course.

**So what.** The draft's old/modern mechanism (Weeks 5, 19, 20, 22) is genuinely sourceable on three
independent grounds (GALGO, graded-reader convention, legal-Spanish/notarial precedent); it is not
invented. But the mechanism carries a real, previously unflagged gap: applying LECOLE specifically to
archaic/Golden Age text (as the draft's Week 19 implies) is a plausible but unvalidated extension of a
tool built and tested for a different kind of text, not a directly evidenced application. Separately,
an unabridged Don Quixote excerpt is a weaker Week 19 choice than a shorter Cervantes short story, on
independent graded-reader-pedagogy grounds. The archaic-Spanish-legal-plus-parallel-English-historical-
legal pairing at Weeks 5 and 20 is a novel synthesis this project is making, not one directly precedented
in a general (non-specialist) course context; it is not contradicted by any source, only unprecedented.

**Now what.** The revised syllabus keeps the four-week old/modern mechanism unchanged in structure but
adds: an explicit LECOLE-contemporary-only caveat Phase 6 authors must state rather than imply away; a
concrete, better-evidenced Week 19 default (a shorter Cervantes short story, not Don Quixote); and an
explicit "novel, un-precedented (not contradicted) synthesis" label for the Weeks 5/20 archaic-legal
pairing, so Phase 6 authors present it honestly rather than as a directly precedented design.

## Sub-decision E: core-band interpreting/translation depth

**What.** The CEFR mediation disclaimer (Sub-decision A, [S3]) supports treating light translation/
mediation as legitimate general-course content. Separately, structured consecutive/simultaneous
interpreting training is documented almost exclusively in professional/certification literature; no
general, non-certification B2/C1 course was found in this research teaching structured simultaneous or
consecutive interpreting as standard content [S18, moderate confidence]. A direct cross-check of the
anchor research's own footnotes for its C1-C2 "content-based/project-driven... integrated translation
and interpreting" claim found that those footnotes cite Middlebury Institute of International Studies
(MIIS), Rikkyo University, and the University of Geneva's Faculté de traduction et d'interprétation
(FTI) — graduate professional translation/interpreting degree programs, not general-course pedagogy
[S19, high confidence, this project's own local self-citation].

**So what.** This finding does not make the anchor research's broader content-based/project-driven
design claim false; independent TBLT/CLIL/PBLL evidence (Sub-decision B) supports multi-week thematic
project design for a general advanced course on its own, separate basis. But it does mean the specific
depth the anchor names for C1/C2 interpreting content ("structured training in simultaneous and
consecutive interpreting," "advanced conference interpreting") is itself sourced from professional-track
literature and generalized into this general course's own scaffolding table, without that generalization
being previously flagged. The syllabus draft's pre-existing hedges at Weeks 12, 24, and 25
("core-band-appropriate," "orientation") were already directionally correct but not explicitly grounded
in this citation-scope finding.

**Now what.** The revised syllabus strengthens, rather than newly introduces, those hedges: Weeks 12,
24, and 25 must keep interpreting content at sight-translation and consecutive-adjacent orientation
depth, and must not build simultaneous-interpreting-booth exercises (equipment-based, real-time
dual-channel practice), which remain out of core-band scope and belong only to the separate Phase 7
professional track.

## Confirms/extends/contradicts the anchor documents

**Confirms.** LECOLE is real and correctly cited by the anchor research. The CEFR global-scale wording
the anchor research paraphrases is accurate. Instituto Cervantes' own Plan Curricular directly confirms
the anchor's C1-C2 professional/academic/literary characterization. "Content-based and project-driven"
is a legitimate broad design category for C1-C2, independently supported by TBLT/CLIL/PBLL evidence,
not solely by the anchor's own assertion.

**Extends.** This briefing adds specifics the anchor research and `curriculum-architecture.md` do not
themselves supply: the CEFR Companion Volume's expanded mediation-scale framework; concrete unit-
duration and continuity-mechanic evidence from CLIL (6-8 weeks) and Cervantes AVE Global (approximately
10-hour blocks); the finding that pluricentric dialect-weighting methodology is an underdeveloped field
with no available weighting methodology (reinforcing, not creating, the syllabus's existing dialect-
weighting Unknown flag); and the LECOLE-contemporary-only validation-scope caveat.

**Tensions (not outright contradictions).** The anchor research's own C1-C2 interpreting/translation
claims are sourced from professional-interpreter-training-program literature, then generalized to a
general course's own scaffolding table without that generalization being flagged in the anchor document
itself. Separately, the observable market pattern (AVE Global and SIELE both omit or avoid C2) sits in
tension with — though does not contradict the legitimacy of — this project's own commitment to a fully
symmetric, fully-authored C2 half.

## Assumptions, unknowns, and contradictions

- **Assumption.** This briefing treats CLIL's documented 6-8-week module pilot and Cervantes AVE
  Global's approximately 10-hour topic blocks as reasonable proxies for "bounded multi-week thematic
  block" sizing, even though neither source was built for a bilingual Spanish-English professional
  curriculum specifically. This is an analogical inference, not a directly matching precedent.
- **Unknown, not resolvable by further desk research.** Pluricentric Spanish-teaching dialect-weighting
  methodology: no source reviewed offers a documented methodology for weighting dialect-zone coverage
  across a multi-variety curriculum. This briefing treats this as definitively thin evidence, not a gap
  further research is likely to close.
- **Unknown, requires instructor decision, not further research.** Whether the project wants
  content-style multi-week arcs (in-scope, no tooling change) or true PBLL-fidelity cumulative
  deliverables (build-affecting, requires new engineering scope). This briefing supplies the evidence
  needed to understand the tradeoff; it does not and cannot resolve which the instructor prefers.
- **Contradiction risk flagged, not found.** No source reviewed directly contradicts the syllabus
  draft's Weeks 5/20 archaic-legal-plus-parallel-English-historical-legal synthesis; it is labeled
  novel/un-precedented specifically because the evidence is silent on it, not because any source argues
  against it.
- **Access limitation.** Several primary Council of Europe URLs (notably the CEFR Companion Volume PDF)
  returned HTTP 403 during this research session; Companion Volume specifics in this briefing rely on
  secondary academic summaries at paraphrase-level confidence, not verbatim primary-source confidence.
- **Freshness note.** `docs/research/inclusive-spanish-usage.md` was not re-fetched or re-verified
  during this session; this briefing does not touch or revise that document's own findings.

## Validation measures

- Before Phase 6 authors write Week 19's actual excerpt, they should independently verify the specific
  Cervantes short-story text chosen against a current, citable Golden Age glossing resource (for
  example GALGO or an equivalent scholarly gloss), since this briefing recommends the short-story
  format on pedagogical grounds, not a specific verified text.
- Before Phase 6 authors write Weeks 12, 24, and 25's interpreting exercises, they should confirm the
  planned activity design against the "sight-translation and consecutive-adjacent orientation depth,
  no simultaneous-interpreting booth" boundary this briefing establishes, ideally with a second reviewer
  pass specifically checking for scope creep toward Phase 7-depth content.
- If a Phase 6 author or the instructor needs verbatim CEFR Companion Volume mediation-scale wording,
  that text should be independently re-sourced (a direct, successful fetch or a licensed copy), since
  this briefing's Companion Volume claims rest on secondary summaries only.
- The 13/13 pacing split and the curated/accelerated contact-hour positioning statement both require
  explicit instructor confirmation; neither is validated by external research alone, since no official
  guided-hour benchmark exists to validate against.
