# Advanced Spanish Dialect-Weighting Methodology: Evidence-Graded Candidates for the Lizbeth Spanish Advanced Band

## Purpose and how this briefing was commissioned

This briefing was commissioned to answer, non-arbitrarily, a specific unresolved question the
Advanced-band syllabus and its governing plan both flagged as Unknown: how many times each of the
project's ten required Spanish dialect zones/locations (Mexico, Argentina, Colombia, Peru, the
Dominican Republic, Ecuador, Puerto Rico, El Salvador, Honduras, Spain) should be featured across the
26-week C1-to-C2 Advanced band, replacing the ad-hoc "each location at least twice, roughly even"
default that produced an uneven outcome by the end of the Intermediate band (Mexico, Honduras, and
Colombia each received three passes; seven other locations received two; El Salvador received exactly
one). This question directly feeds a downstream adversarial review that will pick a resolved
methodology for `docs/plans/lizbeth-spanish-advanced-band.md` decision #5.

This briefing extends, rather than repeats, an earlier finding. The prior research briefing
(`docs/research/advanced-spanish-c1-c2-instructional-design.md`) already searched this question and
concluded that pluricentric Spanish-teaching dialect-weighting methodology is "an underdeveloped field
... this briefing treats this as definitively thin evidence, not a gap further research is likely to
close." This briefing's deeper, comparison-mode search confirms that conclusion at the level of a
ready-made, purpose-built method, but shows the design space is nonetheless populatable: real,
gradeable evidence exists that bears on the choice, even though no single source hands over a finished
allocation rule.

Audience: the instructor (course owner, for the decision this briefing feeds) and the reviewer
conducting the downstream adversarial review of the resulting decision.

**Authority boundary.** This briefing is advisory research evidence. It does not itself decide the
dialect-weighting question; it supplies evidence-graded candidate methodologies and their groundings so
that the plan's decision #5 can resolve the question in a separate, citing document
(`docs/plans/lizbeth-spanish-advanced-band.md`), which in turn directs the corresponding update to
`lizbeth_spanish/curriculum/advanced-band-syllabus.md`.

## Decision context

The Advanced-band syllabus rotates dialect-zone coverage across ten required locations over
approximately 20-21 location-anchored week-slots (20 single- or paired-anchor weeks, one of which,
Week 17, pairs two locations). The current working default gives every location at least two passes and
gives Spain three, on the stated rationale that Spain is where the syllabus's old/modern register
mechanism structurally concentrates (Golden Age literature and historical legal register are both
conventionally Peninsular-associated bodies of content). Neither the governing plan nor the syllabus has
so far had an evidence-backed answer for whether "floor of two, with author-judgment exceptions" is
itself defensible, or whether some other weighting principle (population, diaspora/learner-relevance,
or business/economic relevance) should govern instead. This briefing was asked to evidence-grade the
available design space, not to make the final call.

## What the evidence shows

**No purpose-built quantitative dialect-weighting methodology exists in the pedagogy literature
[S1][S2][S3][S4], inference, high confidence.** Pluricentric-language pedagogy — Clyne's pluricentricity
framework, World Englishes scholarship, and Instituto Cervantes' own Plan Curricular del Instituto
Cervantes (PCIC) — addresses which variety should serve as the base instructional norm, whether learners
should be exposed to variety diversity at all, and attitudes toward and stigma around non-standard
varieties. None of it addresses how much relative airtime each variety should receive across a course.
This confirms the prior briefing's "no available weighting methodology" finding at the level of a
ready-made method, but the deeper search summarized below shows the design space can still be
populated with gradeable partial evidence.

**PCIC's own inclusion criterion is qualitative, and PCIC explicitly endorses learner-context-driven
calibration over any fixed allocation rule [S2], observation, high confidence.** PCIC uses center-north
Peninsular *norma culta* as its base reference, and its stated criterion for including another variety's
features is qualitative: a feature must be "sufficiently general, easy to perceive, wide area of use and
validity," not derived from any frequency or allocation rule. Critically, PCIC directs teachers to
calibrate variety emphasis to "their teaching context, the speaking community in which [learners] are
immersed, and the interests and perspectives of learners." This is the strongest authority-backed
rationale available for deliberately uneven, context-driven coverage rather than a flat, even, or
population-derived default — but it is conditioned on the teacher actually knowing something specific
about the learner's context and interests.

**SLA frequency and spaced-repetition research is robust and directly adaptable as a floor principle,
though its transfer to dialect-zone features is an analogy, not a direct finding [S5][S6].** The
vocabulary-acquisition literature is observed at high confidence to show that learners need
approximately 8-12 meaningful encounters for a stable representation and approximately 20-30 spaced
repetitions for durable long-term retention; recall falls below roughly 30% with fewer than six spaced
encounters, versus over 80% at ten or more encounters, and shorter inter-encounter lags aid retention.
Applying this floor logic to consolidating a dialect zone's distinctive features, rather than single
vocabulary items, is an inference by analogy, moderate confidence, not itself a directly evidenced
claim. It remains the single best-evidenced element available anywhere in this research question, and it
directly indicts the Intermediate band's El Salvador one-pass outcome as falling below any plausible
consolidation threshold.

**Demographic data strongly supports a population-weighted scheme in principle, but the underlying data
skews hard toward Mexico [S7][S8], observation, high confidence.** Mexico has approximately 138 million
native speakers, roughly 21% of all Spanish speakers worldwide; Colombia follows at approximately 53
million, Spain at approximately 49 million, Argentina at approximately 47 million, and Peru at
approximately 34 million, with Ecuador, the Dominican Republic, Honduras, El Salvador, and Puerto Rico
progressively far smaller. A population-proportional allocation applied literally would give Mexico
roughly eight to nine passes across the band and push El Salvador and Puerto Rico toward zero or one
pass — that is, it would starve exactly the small-population zones this project deliberately chose to
include in its ten-location set.

**Population/market weighting is an observed real-world industry practice, but for a different goal than
this course's [S9][S13], observation, high confidence.** Media "neutral Spanish" is explicitly
Mexican-based because of Mexico's "larger territory and bigger population," and language apps default to
Brazilian Portuguese over European Portuguese for the analogous population reason (roughly 200 million
speakers). These are real precedents, but they choose a single broadcast variety for maximum reach — the
opposite of a course whose stated design intent is deliberate multi-variety exposure. The precedent
therefore transfers weakly to this project's actual goal.

**US Hispanic-origin composition gives a concrete diaspora/learner-relevance weighting input, and it
appears to already shape this project's own ten-location set [S10], observation, high confidence; the
location-set inference is moderate-high confidence.** Mexican-origin residents make up 58% of the US
Hispanic population, Puerto Rican-origin 9.6%, and Salvadoran (2.3 million), Dominican (2.2 million),
Colombian (1.3 million), and Honduran (1.1 million) origin populations round out the eight groups
exceeding one million. This project's ten-location list is heavy on US-diaspora-relevant Central
American and Caribbean zones (El Salvador, Honduras, the Dominican Republic, Puerto Rico), consistent
with a heritage/diaspora rationale for location selection. But the list also includes Argentina, Peru,
and Spain (low US-origin share, high prestige/tourism/reference value) and excludes Cuba and Guatemala
(both top-eight US-origin groups), so the location set is best read as a blend of rationales, not a
purely diaspora-driven set.

**Mutual intelligibility across Spanish dialects is high (85-95%), which functions as a meta-constraint
that lowers the stakes of the entire allocation decision [S11], observation, moderate-high confidence.**
All major varieties are mutually intelligible, and a recognized "neutral/standard Spanish" register
exists. Because comprehension rarely fails across varieties, allocation serves cultural breadth and
recognition, not comprehension necessity. This undercuts comprehension-based arguments for heavily
skewing coverage toward any one variety and makes a broadly balanced default a low-risk choice.

**Analogous pluricentric languages debate integration, not allocation ratios [S12][S13], observation,
moderate confidence.** Arabic pedagogy's live debate is Modern Standard Arabic-first versus integrated
MSA-plus-dialect instruction — a sequencing and inclusion question, not an allocation-frequency question.
Portuguese teaching (for example, Cambridge's CULP programme) exposes learners to both European and
Brazilian variants with per-level contrast notes, but publishes no coverage-ratio method either. This
confirms the allocation-frequency gap identified above is cross-linguistic, not specific to Spanish.

**No peer-reviewed study directly ties dialect-exposure frequency to learner outcomes; this is a genuine
evidence gap, high confidence that the gap itself is real.** The frequency-outcome evidence cited above
[S5][S6] concerns vocabulary and grammar items, not dialect-zone features specifically. Its transfer to
dialect-zone-feature consolidation is the single load-bearing inference behind Candidate A's floor
principle, below.

## Candidate methodologies

Four candidate allocation methodologies were evidence-graded, plus one non-candidate the evidence
argues against treating as a fifth option.

### Candidate A — Balanced rotation with an enforced spaced-exposure floor

**Rule.** Every location receives at least two passes; passes for a given zone must be spaced
(non-adjacent) across the band; any remaining slots beyond the floor go to zones with the richest
teachable contrasts or the strongest natural grammar/topic tie-ins, as the project's current draft
already partially does with Spain.

**Grounding.** The floor sub-principle is moderate-to-strong: it rests on robust SLA frequency and
spacing science [S5][S6], applied by direct analogy from vocabulary items to dialect-zone features. The
even-rotation choice itself carries only weak positive evidence, but the risk of choosing it is low
given the 85-95% mutual-intelligibility finding [S11]. It is also the best fit to existing project
practice and to PCIC's stance that all varieties' *norma culta* deserves attention [S2].

**This-project implication.** Over the roughly 20-21 location-anchored weeks, most locations receive two
passes; a small number of zones with the richest genuine content contrasts (in the current draft, only
Spain, via the old/modern mechanism) receive a third pass. This directly and structurally fixes the
Intermediate band's El Salvador one-pass defect, since the floor is now an enforced minimum rather than
an incidental outcome.

**Weaknesses.** The rule disciplines the floor and the spacing, but it does not by itself say *which*
zones earn the ceiling exception; that judgment is deferred to whoever applies the rule, unless
additional grounded criteria (as this briefing supplies below) are also applied.

### Candidate B — Population-weighted

**Rule.** Passes proportional to native-speaker population.

**Grounding.** The underlying data is strong [S7][S8]. The pedagogical-outcome warrant is speculative:
no evidence was found that population-proportional exposure aids learning outcomes specifically. A
real-world precedent exists (media "neutral Spanish," Duolingo's Brazilian-Portuguese default), but for
single-variety broadcast reach, not multi-variety pedagogy [S9][S13].

**This-project implication.** Mexico would receive roughly eight to nine passes; Colombia, Spain, and
Argentina roughly three each; Peru roughly two; Ecuador, the Dominican Republic, and Honduras roughly
one each; El Salvador roughly zero to one; Puerto Rico roughly zero.

**Weaknesses.** This directly starves the small diaspora-relevant zones the project intentionally
included, contradicts the project's own stated multi-variety design intent, and over-concentrates on a
single location. Evidence grade: weak as a pedagogical method, despite the strength of the underlying
population data.

### Candidate C — Diaspora- / learner-relevance-weighted

**Rule.** Passes proportional to the learner population's real-world contact profile, using US
Hispanic-origin composition [S10] as a default proxy, or the specific learner population's stated ties
and goals where known.

**Grounding.** The underlying data is strong [S10]. Institutional endorsement is moderate: PCIC
explicitly endorses calibrating variety emphasis to the learner's immersed speaking community and
interests [S2]. This candidate also best explains the project's existing ten-location choice, which
already skews toward US-diaspora-relevant Central American and Caribbean zones.

**This-project implication.** Under the US-origin proxy, Mexico, Puerto Rico, El Salvador, Honduras,
the Dominican Republic, and Colombia would be boosted; Argentina, Peru, and Spain would be reduced
toward the floor.

**Weaknesses.** This candidate requires a learner-context input — who the course is actually for — that
the research record alone does not supply; the US-origin proxy is an assumption unless the actual
learner population is confirmed to match it. Evidence grade: moderate, and best-aligned to the project's
existing location choice only if the learner context matches the proxy.

### Candidate D — Business/economic-relevance-weighted

**Rule.** Passes proportional to GDP, trade, or business-travel relevance, mapped to the anchor
research's own business verticals (Mexican Spanish for manufacturing, Rioplatense/Argentine Spanish for
finance, Caribbean Spanish for tourism).

**Grounding.** The underlying data is moderate. The pedagogical warrant is weak and context-dependent: it
is only coherent if the band were business-first, which it is not — the Advanced band carries a business
escalation framework, but that framework is one component among four domain arcs (Legal, Finance,
Technology, Literature), not the band's sole purpose.

**This-project implication.** Mexico, Spain, Colombia, Argentina, and Peru would be boosted; El Salvador,
Honduras, and Puerto Rico would be starved, in the same pattern as Candidate B.

**Weaknesses.** Same small-zone starvation risk as Candidate B; only defensible for a business-track
variant of the course, which this course is not. Evidence grade: weak-to-moderate, narrow applicability.

### Non-candidate — dialect-centrality / mutual-intelligibility weighting

The mutual-intelligibility evidence [S11] is better read as a constraint that lowers the stakes of the
whole allocation decision than as a positive weighting method in its own right. It does not yield an
implementable pass-count rule (there is no principled way to convert "85-95% mutually intelligible" into
a specific number of passes for a specific zone), so it is noted here explicitly so the downstream
review does not mistake it for a fifth candidate.

## The honest lopsidedness note

The evidence is genuinely lopsided in one narrow respect only: the spaced minimum-exposure floor (the
disciplined core of Candidate A) is meaningfully better-evidenced than an unstructured author-judgment
default, and it can be adopted on its own merits regardless of which full weighting scheme ultimately
governs the ceiling. It is the direct, evidence-backed answer to "how few passes is too few," and it is
the single strongest, most actionable finding in this entire research question.

The choice among the four full weighting schemes (even/floor-based, population, diaspora, or business)
is not resolvable by evidence alone; it is a values and learner-context decision. The high
mutual-intelligibility finding means any reasonable choice among them carries low downside risk for
comprehension specifically, even if the choice is imperfect on other grounds.

This is a deeper, more actionable conclusion than the prior briefing's flat "no methodology exists," but
it does not overturn that finding's core: no off-the-shelf, validated allocation method exists anywhere
in the literature reviewed. This briefing populates the design space with gradeable candidates; it does
not discover a hidden ready-made answer.

## Unknowns and contradictions

- **Unknown.** No study directly links dialect-exposure frequency to learner outcomes. Candidate A's
  floor principle rests on a vocabulary-to-dialect-feature analogy, not a direct finding.
- **Unknown, input-required.** The specific learner context that would make Candidate C or D concrete was,
  at the time this briefing was researched, unresolved. This has since been resolved for this project:
  the course owner has confirmed the platform's learner base is "a mix" of general adult learners, with
  no dominant regional or heritage tie and no dominant business-only focus. The comparative analysis that
  applies this finding to the four candidates and reaches a recommendation is a separate deliverable
  (`docs/plans/lizbeth-spanish-advanced-band.md`, decision #5), not this briefing; this briefing supplies
  the evidence-graded candidates that analysis weighs, and does not itself perform that weighing.
- **Contradiction handled, not found.** Population/market weighting is real industry practice [S9][S13],
  but for single-variety reach, which is the inverse of this course's stated multi-variety intent. Its
  precedent value for this course is therefore weak, not strong; this is a scope mismatch, not a direct
  contradiction of any single source.
- **Meta-constraint.** The 85-95% mutual-intelligibility finding [S11] lowers the stakes of the entire
  decision and argues against over-engineering a weighting formula beyond what the evidence actually
  supports.

## Research limits

Several primary sources (JSTOR, ResearchGate, PCIC, and US Census pages) were read via search-engine
summaries and snippets during this research session, not full-text fetches; population and US-origin
figures are Statista- and Census-summary level (2020-2025), directionally reliable but not
independently re-derived from primary tables. No peer-reviewed source directly quantifying a
dialect-coverage allocation ratio, and no source directly tying dialect-exposure frequency to learner
outcomes, was found after a genuinely deep search spanning Spanish, English (World Englishes/ELF),
Arabic, and Portuguese pedagogy. The SLA frequency/spacing science cited here is foundational and stable;
demographic data is current to 2020-2025; pluricentric-pedagogy sources span 1992 (Clyne) through
2024-2026.

## Validation measures

- If the course owner's confirmed "a mix" learner-population description changes materially (for
  example, if the platform later observes a concentrated actual learner base skewed toward a specific
  diaspora, region, or business-only segment), Candidates C and D should be re-evaluated against that new
  , more specific context rather than assumed permanently foreclosed.
- Population and US Hispanic-origin figures cited here (2020-2025 vintage) should be refreshed if this
  methodology is revisited more than roughly two to three years after this briefing's publication date.
- The SLA frequency/spacing floor principle (Candidate A's core) should be reviewed against any future
  peer-reviewed study that directly measures dialect-feature exposure frequency against learner outcomes,
  should one be published; none was found as of this briefing's research date.
